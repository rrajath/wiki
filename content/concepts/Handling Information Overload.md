# Handling Information Overload

A three-category system for structuring information intake so that [[Information Overload]] does not degrade attention and decision quality.

## The three categories

| Category | Rule | Why it works |
|----------|------|--------------|
| **Books** | Fix a number per month | Bounded, deliberate depth — you finish what you start |
| **One obsession** | Only one topic at a time | Prevents the scattered feeling of juggling many interests simultaneously; lets you go deep instead of wide |
| **Frivolous** | Schedule a time slot | Newsletters, YouTube, social feeds get a container rather than bleeding into the whole day |

The goal is not zero information consumption — it is *intentional* information consumption. You decide what enters and when, rather than letting the stream flow unchecked.

## Why structure matters

Without active management, [[Our Defaults]] take over: the brain reaches for whatever is most available and stimulating, which is rarely the most useful. The three-category structure replaces reactive scrolling with a deliberate intake plan.

This directly addresses [[Decision Fatigue]]: every "what should I read/watch now?" decision is pre-decided by the system. The mental overhead of choosing is eliminated for lower-stakes categories.

## Relation to awareness

The system only works if you notice when you are drifting outside it. [[Awareness]] — the capacity to observe your own mental state in real time — is what lets you catch yourself reaching for a newsletter during deep-work hours and redirect.
